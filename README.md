# COMP0077 — Signal Strength in Portfolio Construction

Numerical simulations for the MSc Computational Finance dissertation **Mathematical Modelling of Signal Strength in Portfolio Construction**.

**Author:** Ka Shing Law<br>
**Module:** COMP0077 — Numerical Simulation

## Overview

This project studies how imperfect investment signals should be translated into portfolio weights and how strongly those signals should be acted upon. Equal weight is used as the no-information benchmark, and portfolio performance is evaluated using the mean–variance certainty equivalent relative to that benchmark.

Two preliminary allocation maps are examined:

1. The **Gibbs softmax map**, a multiplicative, long-only tilt,

   $$
   w_i(\boldsymbol{s},\beta)=\frac{e^{\beta s_i}}{\sum_j e^{\beta s_j}}.
   $$

2. The **combination map**, an additive tilt that may include short positions,

   $$
   \boldsymbol{w}(\alpha)=(1-\alpha)\boldsymbol{w}_{\mathrm{eq}}+\alpha\boldsymbol{w}_{\mathrm{sig}},
   \qquad
   \boldsymbol{w}_{\mathrm{sig}}=\frac{\Sigma^{-1}\boldsymbol{s}}{\boldsymbol{1}^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\boldsymbol{s}}.
   $$

The general-framework notebook then connects the two maps through an entropy-regularised mean–variance programme and studies how the objective, feasible set, signal content, signal form and signal precision affect portfolio construction.

## Chapter 3: preliminary approaches

The Chapter 3 notebooks use one-step geometric Brownian motion and common random numbers to compare portfolios draw by draw.

### Softmax experiments

- Sweep the softmax strength $\beta$ and locate the mean–variance CE maximiser.
- Increase drift-signal noise and examine how both the optimal strength and peak performance change.
- Introduce equicorrelated and three-factor Brownian increments while keeping signal errors independent of realised returns.

### Combination experiments

- Sweep the additive strength $\alpha$, recording short exposure and gross leverage.
- Vary drift-signal quality and examine sensitivity to the normaliser-domain rule.
- Introduce heterogeneous volatilities and compare drift and volatility input errors at matched information levels.
- Test reliability-motivated shrinkage of both inputs and the identification of their separate loadings.
- Compare the combination and softmax maps under common and heterogeneous volatility with correlated returns.

## Chapter 4: general framework

The general-framework notebook contains six experiments:

1. Compare the softmax edge, the entropy-regularised interior, the sparse long-only boundary and the budget hyperplane.
2. Test whether a portfolio map benefits from a market-level signal that contains no cross-sectional ranking information.
3. Relate drift-signal precision to an outcome-informed top-$k$ benchmark.
4. Compare cardinal, rank-based and binary forms of the same drift signal.
5. Combine two drift signals with correlated forecast errors and compare the simulated loading with the Gaussian GLS benchmark.
6. Measure how closely noisy-input maps recover the tangency and global minimum-variance portfolios, and compare recovery-optimal with CE-optimal strength.

## Numerical design

The main simulations use $N=20$ assets, a one-year horizon, $30{,}000$ Monte-Carlo replications and risk aversion $\gamma=5$. Unless an experiment explicitly changes the setting, the common volatility is $0.20$, the mean drift is $0.05$, and both cross-sectional drift dispersion and baseline drift-signal noise are $0.05$.

Portfolio performance is measured by

$$
\mathrm{CE}_{MV}(P)=\mathbb{E}[P]-\frac{\gamma}{2}\operatorname{Var}(P),
$$

and reported in basis points relative to equal weight. The combination map has a singular normaliser when $\boldsymbol{1}^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\boldsymbol{s}$ approaches zero, so its experiments state an explicit retention rule and use common intersection samples for comparisons.

## Usage

Start Jupyter from the repository directory:

```bash
jupyter notebook
```

The notebooks can then be opened and run independently. For the order used in the dissertation, run:

1. `Efficient_Frontier.ipynb`
2. `softmax_simulation.ipynb`
3. `combination_simulation.ipynb`
4. `general_framework.ipynb`

Fixed random seeds are used throughout so that the reported simulations and figures can be reproduced.

## Scope

The project is simulation-based and aims to identify structural behaviour under controlled calibrations. It does not validate the allocation maps against historical returns, and numerical conclusions should be interpreted within the parameter sets and normaliser rules stated in each experiment.
